      Under the hood

      Four passes between your photo and a room you would build.

      01

      It reads the room

      A vision model surveys the photograph and writes down what is in it — every piece of furniture, how many there are, and where the doors and windows sit. Two single beds come back as two, not one double, which is the difference between a design of your room and a design of a room.

      02

      You say what stays

      Everything it found starts as something that must still be there. Let the bed go if you want a new bed; keep the desk if you work at it. Doors, windows, the ceiling height and the camera position are never yours to lose — those are the building, not the furnishing.

      03

      It redraws the whole photograph

      Not a patch over part of it. The image model is given the entire room, at its real proportions, and told in detail what must survive: this wardrobe is a wardrobe and never a doorway, the air conditioner stays on the wall it is bolted to, one bed remains one bed.

      04

      It prices the work

      The same reading becomes an itemised range — storage, finishes, lighting, labour — so the picture arrives with a number beside it. Indicative, not a quotation, and it says so.

      Earlier versions masked the doors and windows and let the generator repaint everything else. That erased the furniture it was never told about, so the protection moved into words, where it can name a wardrobe rather than draw a box round it.
